Most HR and knowledge workers think building AI agents requires coding skills.

It doesn't.

I recently completed Microsoft's Agent Academy Recruit curriculum—13 missions taking you from zero to deploying functional AI agents in Microsoft 365 without writing traditional code.

The training covers agent fundamentals (conversational vs autonomous), Copilot Studio's four building blocks (Knowledge, Tools, Topics, Instructions), and how RAG (Retrieval-Augmented Generation) grounds responses in your actual documents rather than hallucinating.

You'll build declarative agents, learn solution packaging for lifecycle management, deploy pre-built templates, and create custom agents using only natural language prompts. Later missions cover topics, triggers, adaptive cards, and publishing.

The pattern that emerges:
1. Describe your agent's purpose in plain language
2. Add knowledge sources (SharePoint, documents, websites)
3. Define guardrails in the instructions
4. Test with the Activity Map
5. Publish to Teams or M365 Copilot

The curriculum is designed for makers, not developers.

---

While exploring this, I came across Nate Jones' idea that flips the script entirely.

You see a role posted three hours ago with 400 applicants. Most people optimise their CV keywords to beat the ATS. Jones argues this is optimising for a robot whose only job is to filter people out.

His alternative: build a Personal Search Engine—an interactive "Digital Twin" of your professional brain. A hiring manager lands on your site and instead of scrolling bullet points, they query a chat interface: "Have you led a team through a crisis?" The AI answers using your actual experience, with citations.

The technology is RAG—the same concept from Agent Academy. And "Vibe Coding" with tools like Lovable means you can build this in a weekend without engineering skills.

The strategy: Don't say "Here's my CV." Say: "I built an AI interface trained on my background. Query my experience directly to see if I'm a match."

Words are cheap. Building is proof of work.
